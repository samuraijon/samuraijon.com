Alpe d'Huez, eight years apart

2016202421 hairpins

My first trip to Europe in 2016 ended at the foot of the Alpe with a bike I'd flown over from Brisbane. In 2024 I went back to ride it again.

The full story

In the third year of my PhD I presented at the European Society of Biomechanics in Lyon and brought my bike along. With tips from friends at UQCC I managed to see the Tour de France, then rode the climbs I'd only watched on TV.

I like to ride my bicycle

A grade with UQCCWTOS DelftRWC Ahoy

Cycling lets me chase perfection in every detail, from a spotless, dialled-in bike to a colour-coordinated kit. All my bikes are white or baby blue.

How it started

I started riding to get to uni after seeing a friend's road bike. My first was an old blue Giant TCR, which I hid at uni for a few days before explaining it at home. It was stolen from a broken carport gate a few months later, but by then I'd caught the bug: a Specialized Allez, then UQCC, racing from C grade up to A grade, and a Venge and an Izalco along the way.

    Wanderlust

    Growing up in Taiwan, South Africa and Australia gave me a love for travel that never left. Every coloured-in country, every ride and every flight path is a memory. The interactive maps from my old site come back here, linked to Strava.

    Languages

    Growing up in different places, I had to make myself understood. Now languages are how I get to know the places I ride through. I see them as patterns, which makes them easier to learn.

    How I learn them

    Use whatever vocabulary you have and don't be afraid of mistakes. I immerse myself in pop culture: Spanish music, and Instagram reels in Spanish and French, mostly about cycling and cats. One day I'd like to add Italian and Portuguese, and make videos comparing them.

    Other interests

    Cats, bananas, coffee, cooking, baking, playing the guitar and piano, and a bit of running.

    I also fly drones. I'm a certified EU A1/A3 open category drone pilot, and I love filming rides and mountain roads from above.
